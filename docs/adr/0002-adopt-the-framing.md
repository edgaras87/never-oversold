# 0002. Adopt the framing as the project's truth set

Date: 2026-09-10
Status: Accepted

## Context

The briefing arrived on 2026-09-09 and opened framing: inventory
reservation under contention, one claim to be proven for a reader
judging correctness-first construction. The method
(`docs/concept/03-cbc-framing.md`) was run jointly, the reviewer
giving every verdict, in the derivation order it prescribes —
promise, what must be ours, the environment, the collisions, the
owners, between owners, the slice surface — with the working
record kept at `docs/system/framing/derivation.md`, every step
frozen at its verdict and revised only by logged return trips.

Framing may honestly end in "no project". It did not: the promise
cleared the six tests the briefing set, four settled on the
sentence and two carried to their proof points.

## Options considered

1. **Adopt the three exports as the project's truth set** — the
   intent, the system definition, the slice registry under
   `docs/system/`, composed from the derivation record and standing
   alone; the record kept beside them as the archive.
2. **End in "no project"** — the method's valid exit. Rejected: the
   promise is falsifiable, stageable, and worth proving to the
   named reader; nothing in the census or the collisions found the
   territory empty or the claim unkeepable.
3. **Frame a wider intent** carrying request identity beside
   contention. Rejected: two claims, two adversity classes — an
   overloaded intent, and outside the briefing's bar. Banked in the
   intent; the route for it is the next project or a dated
   revision after release.
4. **Keep the exports at the repository root** with dotted names,
   as an earlier run once did by default. Rejected: `docs/system/`
   makes the truth set one nameable path, and the directory carries
   what a prefix would.

## Decision

Option 1. The three files under `docs/system/` are the project's
truth: what it promises, what it owns and refuses, what attacks it
and what dies, who owns it, and the work cut into four slices with
SL-1 chosen-next. The README is derived from them and never
authored on its own. After this close they change only through a
dated revision entry in each file's log — the registry's statuses
at every slice close, the other two when later work changes what
they claim. The derivation record stays as the archive; nothing
cites it as the master.

The hand-off is to the ground and the bootstrap, never directly to
slicing: infrastructure stood up to the registry's needs, then an
empty-but-running skeleton with the evidence harness proven on
SL-1's adversity.

## Consequences

Good: one claim, in writing, before any code or technology choice;
every later decision can be traced to a line in these three files;
what was refused is written, so a later "why not X" finds its
answer instead of reopening the debate.

Bad: the exports are living records and must be kept true by
dated entries, a discipline that costs attention at every slice
close; the promise is narrow by design, and a reader wanting
request identity or durability guarantees finds them refused, with
reasons, rather than delivered.
